## B1 — blocking
Claim: §4 keys M3's search extent on a binary over **manifest presence** and then draws its universal from a different predicate — **manifest locatability** — one sentence apart, so the document supports two implementable readings of the unnamed third case that give different exits on the same file, and it retires AC-DT-10 for M3 under both.
Location: specs/directive-tooling.md §4, the M3 row (`specs/directive-tooling.md:676`); §4, "Where there is no manifest, M3 searches the whole file" (`:725`, `:738`); §4, "Two consequences" (`:747-750`); §4 Non-functional goals, Reliability (`:1198`); §6 AC-DT-06 (`:1554`).
Evidence: Verified by reading, at the pinned commit. The M3 row states the binary on presence: "where the directive carries a G11 manifest the search runs over the disposition author region **as the manifest locates it** … where it carries **no manifest** the search runs over the whole file" (*observed*, `:676`). Twelve lines later the §4 block states the premise on locatability: M3 "is decidable that way on any directive **whose manifest names the disposition author region**" (*observed*, `:725`) — and four lines after that draws the universal: "the check has a stated extent on **every** directive" (*observed*, `:738`). Those are not the same predicate. The M3 row draws the same universal — "M3 is therefore decidable on **every** directive, and no directive makes it an element the lint cannot decide (AC-DT-10)" — and §4's consequences paragraph makes it normative: "**AC-DT-10's undecidability rule no longer reaches M3**: there is no directive on which M3 has no extent to search" (*observed*, `:747-750`). That the gap is reachable is the document's own statement: extent is "computable by reading the landed file together with the manifest it carries, **without knowing what the generator emitted**" (*observed*, §4 G11), and §2 has the lint handed the landed file and nothing else — so the manifest the lint reads carries no guarantee of conforming to G11. Neither binary exists and no manifest was inspected (see *Not inspected*); this is a reading of the document.
Consequence: Take a generated skeleton whose author removes the empty disposition slot — stating the working tree in the task-specific middle instead — and removes its manifest entry with it, leaving the prompt region and the rest of the manifest in place. Two readings of the M3 row are available and the document selects neither. **Reading (i), "manifest" means any manifest**: branch one applies, the manifest does not locate the author region, M3 has no extent, and M3 is an element the lint cannot decide — which AC-DT-10 requires to be reported unknown at a non-zero exit, and which `:747` says AC-DT-10 no longer reaches. An implementer building to `:747` writes no branch for the case at all, and its behaviour is then whatever falls out: an empty extent exits non-zero (safe), and a "nothing located, nothing to check" branch exits **0** — a silent pass on a directive whose disposition is exactly where the motivating incident put it. **Reading (ii), "a G11 manifest" means a conforming one**: the file carries no conforming manifest, branch two applies, and the whole file is searched — but this file *does* carry a generator-written prompt region, which is the one thing §4 says the fallback's ground excludes ("a manifest-less directive carries no generator-written prompt region", `:729`). The prompt names both forms, so M3 counts two and exits non-zero on a directive stating exactly one — the §7 paste residual biting a directive nobody pasted, and the cost cycle 12's N1 option (b) named. Reading (ii) also requires the lint to validate a manifest against G11 to decide which branch applies, which is strictly harder than locating one region and which no criterion states. So: two implementers build to §6, disagree on the exit, and both satisfy the document — the shape cycle 8's B2 was blocking for. Separately, `:747` contradicts two other statements of this document for this case: AC-DT-10 ("An element the lint cannot decide is reported unknown and exits non-zero"), and the Reliability NFR at `:1198` ("A lint that cannot decide an element reports it unknown and exits non-zero, rather than passing it"). Cycle 12 held this at non-blocking on the ground that "AC-DT-10 governs, non-zero" — that ground is unavailable, because the sentence under review is the one declaring AC-DT-10 not to reach M3. §6 is unharmed: AC-DT-06's own claim is scoped to its fixture set ("**The searched extent is defined on every fixture in this set**", `:1554`) and is true, and no fixture is of this shape — the defect is in §4's universal and in what it retires, not in the criteria.
Fix: Two options; the choice is Dave's, and neither is taken here. **(a) Key the binary on locatability and keep AC-DT-10 live.** M3 runs region-scoped where the manifest **locates** the disposition author region, whole-file where the directive carries **no** manifest, and is **undecidable** — AC-DT-10, unknown, non-zero — where a manifest is present but does not locate it. That is total by construction, needs no change to `:725`'s premise, keeps the fallback's ground true as written, and costs one clause at `:676`, one at `:738`, and the deletion of "AC-DT-10's undecidability rule no longer reaches M3" at `:747`. It is the option this reviewer would propose, **clearly labelled as a proposal**. **(b) Narrow the universal and say so.** Keep the presence binary, replace "every directive" at `:676` and `:738` with the locatability-qualified form, restore AC-DT-10 as M3's fallback for the remainder, and add the case to §7's accepted list. Either way, `:747` cannot stand as written, and whichever is taken, AC-DT-06 should gain a fixture of the third shape so the choice is pinned by a test rather than by prose — which is what cycle 8's B2 fix did for pattern 3.

## N1 — non-blocking
Claim: AC-DT-02 defines its "structural" line class twice and calls the two definitions equivalent; they are not equivalent, and they disagree on exactly the example the criterion's own prose names as covered.
Location: specs/directive-tooling.md §6 AC-DT-02 (`specs/directive-tooling.md:1415-1423`); the prose naming the covered cases (`:1461-1462`).
Evidence: Verified by running. The criterion defines structural first over the **normalized** line — "when it normalizes to a non-empty string every character of which is drawn from the set" — and then restates it over the **raw** line — "equivalently, when the line as it stands in the committed source consists solely of whitespace and at least one character from that set" (*observed*, `:1415-1422`). Normalization is fixed elsewhere in the same criterion as "strip leading and trailing whitespace … and collapse internal runs of whitespace to one space" (*observed*), so a normalized line retains internal single spaces, and a space is not in the set. The two formulations therefore disagree on every line mixing whitespace with set characters. I enumerated the disagreement over `docs/global-context/core.md`, `docs/global-context/decision-layer.md`, `skills/directive-authoring.md`, and this spec by applying both clauses line by line: the three governed sources currently contain **no** diverging line (they contain no markdown table at all — `grep -c '^|'` returns 0 for each), and this spec contains **two**, one of which is its own M-element table delimiter `| --- | --- | --- | --- |` at `:673` and the other the criterion's own set-enumeration line at `:1419`. The criterion's three worked examples (`**`, `1.`, `<!-- -->`) do not discriminate between the clauses; the prose at `:1462` does, naming "a table rule of hyphens and pipes" among the markers the clause reaches, which is true under the raw-line clause and false under the normalized-line clause.
Consequence: The stated intent is the raw-line clause, and the definition given first — the one an implementer reads as the definition — is the other one. Two implementations of AC-DT-02 then disagree: one excludes table delimiters and thematic breaks such as `- - -` and `* * *` from the static check's range, the other does not, so a generator holding the literal `"| --- | --- |"` — a natural thing for a generator emitting its own manifest as a table — reds the static guard on G1 under one reading and passes under the other. That is the "decidable but not satisfiable" residual the cycle-9 gate's N1 named, reintroduced in narrower form, and the ambiguity is precisely the property cycle 11's N1 disposition was dictated to remove ("two implementations could disagree at the margins"). The divergence set over the currently-named sources is **empty today**, which is why this is not blocking; it does not stay empty by construction, because Q1 is open on where the invariant text lives and a table is among the shapes a machine-readable block would take.
Fix: Delete "equivalently" and keep one clause. The raw-line clause is the one the criterion's own prose requires, since it is the one under which "a table rule of hyphens and pipes" is structural: state structural as "when the line as it stands in the committed source consists solely of whitespace and at least one character from that set", and drop the normalized-line formulation. If the normalized-line clause is intended instead, `:1462` must stop naming table rules, and the criterion should say that a multi-token structural line is within the check's range.

## N2 — non-blocking
Claim: The manifest is now an **input contract for the lint**, and no goal, NFR, or acceptance criterion states that the lint reads it, what it must recognize, or what it does with a manifest it cannot parse — while G11 explicitly defers the marker syntax to the TRD.
Location: specs/directive-tooling.md §4 G11 (`specs/directive-tooling.md:592-623`, esp. `:622` "Which marker syntax is used is a TRD question"); §4 M3 row (`:676`); §4 "What M8 claims" (`:900-909`); §6 AC-DT-05, AC-DT-06's M3 clause, AC-DT-19.
Evidence: Verified by reading. G11 is stated entirely as a property of **what the generator emits**, and closes "Which marker syntax is used is a TRD question; that every region carries one, and that the manifest names it, is not" (*observed*). AC-DT-05 binds the generator. Since cycle 11 the lint's M3 takes its search extent "over the disposition author region **as G11's manifest locates it**" (*observed*, `:676`, AC-DT-06, AC-DT-03), and since cycle 12 the lint must additionally decide whether a manifest is present at all in order to select a branch. Both are lint-side reads of a format no goal assigns to the lint. `grep` over §4's lint goals G5–G10 and over §6's AC-DT-06 through AC-DT-13 finds no statement that the lint parses the manifest; §2's use-case list and §4's Portability NFR ("depends on `git` and a checkout of this repository") name no manifest dependency either (*observed*).
Consequence: Two TRD-stage decisions — the generator's marker syntax and the lint's manifest parser — must agree, and nothing in the PRD requires them to or names the coupling as a boundary. Concretely, an implementer building `bin/check-directive` first, before `bin/directive` exists, has no stated format to build against and no criterion that would fail if they guessed wrong; and the PRD gives no answer for a manifest that is present but unparseable, which is the same hole B1 names one level up. It also weakens two claims that read as independent of the generator: §4's "extent is computable … without the generator being present" is true of a human reading the file, but the lint's version of it now depends on an agreement between the two binaries that the document does not state. AC-DT-19's "reaches a verdict on every element M1–M8" is verifiable only once that agreement exists.
Fix: State the coupling where the PRD can carry it: a lint-side goal or a clause in G11 saying that the manifest is a machine-readable input to `bin/check-directive` as well as an output of `bin/directive`, that the two tools share one manifest format, and that a manifest the lint cannot parse is an undecidable input under AC-DT-10 rather than an absent manifest under the fallback. The marker syntax itself can stay a TRD question; that both tools read the same one is not.
