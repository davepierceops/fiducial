## B1 — blocking
Claim: `files` is `[]` on FM-7 and FM-8 — the only two failure paths on which a commit exists — and on those two paths the report carries neither the per-file results AC-LAND-07 requires for each file in the commit nor any label marking them unknown, because §5.2's disclaimer that `[]` is not a claim about the commit is scoped to the paths where no commit was made.
Location: specs/bin-land-trd.md §5.2, the "An empty `files` list where no commit was made" paragraph (line 761); §3.7, the "`files` is exempt" bullet (line 482) and the step-10 clause of "what a step returns" (line 455); §6, the FM-7 and FM-8 rows (lines 916–917); §5.4, AC-LAND-T01; specs/bin-land.md §4 G6 and §6 AC-LAND-07, AC-LAND-09.
Evidence: Inferred by reading at `3412e258`, with provenance verified by running. §3.7 sources `files` from step 10 alone — "step 10 returns the head `ls-remote` reported for `<branch>` as `remote_head`, and then the per-file results" — and step 10 does not run on FM-7 (the push failed at step 9) and does not reach the per-file comparison on FM-8 (the `ls-remote` equality failed first). So `facts` carries no per-file entries and `build` emits `[]`. §5.2's disclaimer names its own scope in its first sentence — "On FM-1 through FM-6, and on FM-11, no commit exists" — and FM-7 and FM-8 are the complement of that set among the detected modes. The two rows are byte-identical at `47758d14`, `03c52191`, and `97888a8` (*observed*, `git show` on each, compared), so this is inherited rather than introduced this cycle. On the PRD side, G6's fourth field is "one result for each file in the commit" and AC-LAND-07 restates it unconditionally over "the output" — "one per-file blob-match result for each file in the commit ... each labelled *observed* or *unknown*" (*observed*, read at `3412e258`) — with no clause restricting it to the success path. AC-LAND-09 adds "On any failure the output names what was established and what was not, as separate statements" (*observed*). The paths of the commit **were** established on both modes: the tool staged them at step 7 and committed them at step 8.
Consequence: On FM-7 the report reads `head: {observed}`, `verification: incomplete`, `files: []`. A session handed that report — which is exactly what PRD §7 says will happen, since FM-7 is the accepted risk — cannot tell from it which files are in the local commit it must now resolve by hand, and the `[]` is indistinguishable in shape from FM-5's `[]`, where an empty staged set is the established answer. §5.2 rests that distinction on the stage token and then says so only for the no-commit paths, so on these two the reader has an unqualified empty list on a commit that is not empty. Worse for the gate: AC-LAND-T01, the criterion this cycle's predecessor strengthened, enumerates FM-7 and FM-8 and asserts key presence, the class domain, and the three value-domain rules — all of which `[]` satisfies — so the divergence ships untested, and a test author working from AC-LAND-07 and an implementer working from §3.7 produce incompatible artifacts on the one failure the PRD tells the tool to expect. This is the two-agents-disagree class §3.7 exists to remove, in the section that claims to remove it.
Fix: State what `files` carries where a commit exists and the comparison did not run. §5.2's own machinery already permits the honest form and §3.7 already names it — "unknown-ness is carried per entry, or by the absence of entries" — so the entry route is available without a new type: on FM-7 and FM-8, one entry per path in the commit with `match: null` and `class: "unknown"`, which satisfies AC-LAND-07's per-file requirement and AC-LAND-09's established/unestablished split at once. The alternative, keeping `[]` and widening §5.2's disclaimer to cover FM-7 and FM-8 explicitly, closes the wording hole but not the AC-LAND-07 one, and it leaves the accepted-risk report unable to name the files it committed; it is the weaker of the two. Either way §3.7's step-10 clause needs a companion sentence saying where the FM-7/FM-8 entries come from, since step 10 is not their source.
Related: N1, O2.

## N1 — non-blocking
Claim: §6's FM-2, FM-3, and FM-4 rows qualify `prior_head` as established only "where the branch existed", while §3.2 step 3, §3.7, §5.2, §6's own FM-9 row, and §9's OQ-4 all have it established unconditionally on every path after step 3 — including the branch-absent arm, where it is the literal `"created"`.
Location: specs/bin-land-trd.md §6, the FM-2, FM-3, and FM-4 rows (lines 911–913); against §3.2 step 3 (lines 149–150), §3.7's "what a step returns" (line 450) and its accumulation rule (line 460), §5.2's `prior_head` value-domain rule (line 743), §6's FM-9 row (line 918), §9's OQ-4 (line 1182); specs/bin-land.md §6, AC-LAND-09.
Evidence: Inferred by reading at `3412e258`, with provenance verified by running. §3.2 step 3 states both arms without a conditional: "where it is absent, `main`'s head is the base and the prior-head field reads `created` (PRD G1, first arm)". §3.7 gives step 3's returns as "`base` and `prior_head`" with no qualifier and states that "the sequence accumulates `facts` across steps and halts at the first `ok: False`" — so on FM-2, FM-3, and FM-4, all of which stop at step 4 or later, step 3 succeeded and its facts are in hand. §5.2 admits `"created"` as a value of `prior_head` with `class: "observed"`, and the shape block at line 724 uses exactly that as its example. Two further passages in the same document read it unconditionally: FM-9's row lists "the prior head" with no qualifier, and OQ-4's argument that G1's stated purpose is satisfied rests on the report "carr[ying] the prior head whenever a step after the branch was resolved fails" — which is false in the branch-absent arm if FM-2/3/4's rows are read as written. **Ruling: contradiction, not wording ambiguity.** The reading that would rescue the rows — that "where the branch existed" modifies the value rather than the establishment — is unavailable, because the same cell in the same table uses the same construction genuinely conditionally three clauses later: FM-3's "the local head of `<branch>` **where that was the failing check**" is a field that really is absent otherwise. A reader has no license to read one "where" as a condition and its neighbour as a gloss. Provenance, verified by running: FM-3 and FM-4 carry the clause byte-identically from `47758d14`; **FM-2 does not** — its cell read "`branch`; `detail.base`." at `47758d14` and gained the clause at cycle 1's `03c52191` (*observed*, `git show` on both, compared). The clause's origin is the PRD: AC-LAND-09 requires the failure output to name "the prior head, **where the branch existed at the remote**" (*observed*), which is the same scoping, lifted.
Consequence: On the branch-absent arm's FM-2, FM-3, and FM-4 — and the branch-absent diverged case is not hypothetical, it is the state §3.4 devotes a subsection to — an implementer building `Report.build` from §3.7 emits `prior_head: {"value": "created", "class": "observed"}`, while a test author building a case from §6's table asserts `class: "unknown"` and `value: null`, and the test fails against a correct implementation. Because this repository separates test authorship from implementation for the same unit of work, that divergence is the expected outcome rather than a risk. The report itself is the second cost: emitting `unknown` where the tool observed the branch's absence is a false negative in the direction PRD G6 exists to prevent — the tool declining to claim a fact it established.
Fix: Delete the qualifier from the three rows, so they read `prior_head` alongside `branch`, and add nothing else — §3.2 step 3 already carries the two-arm rule and §5.2 already carries the value domain. AC-LAND-09 needs no amendment and no PRD cycle: it says the failure output names the prior head "including ... where the branch existed", which is a floor on what must be named, and naming it in both arms exceeds that floor rather than conflicting with it. If the intent was instead to mirror AC-LAND-09's scoping deliberately, say so in the FM-3 paragraph and reconcile OQ-4, whose argument does not survive the mirrored reading.
Related: B1, O2.
