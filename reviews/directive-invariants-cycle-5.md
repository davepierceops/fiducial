## F-1 — non-blocking
Claim: The amendment widened the refs rule to every remote operation but left it inside a region whose paragraph is scoped "Before anything else", while the Stop conditions region's stop-on-any-failed-command trigger carries no exception for it.
Location: `skills/directive-invariants.md:110-114`, against `:150-154`
Evidence: *Observed*, verified by reading, with the failure shape verified by running in this session. The Base verification region reads: "Before anything else, fetch and confirm the base is at the reviewed ref `{{reviewed_ref}}`. Judge every remote operation — fetch, push, ls-remote — by the refs it reports, not by a credential helper's noise on stderr. If the base has moved, stop and report..." The widened sentence is `:111`–`:113`, byte-equal to the amendment's dictated wording (*observed*, 129 bytes, equal). Its subject is now every remote operation, and two of the three it names — push and `ls-remote` — happen at the end of an execution session, not before anything else. The Stop conditions region at `:150`–`:154` is where the executor's escalation trigger lives: "On any failed command, any precondition not met, or any tree mutation you did not intend, including your own — stop and report; do not retry with different flags". It states no exception, names no region, and does not reach the refs rule. The two regions are emitted as separate blocks under separate markers and are read at different moments. The failure this guards is live: in this session `git fetch origin` printed `fatal: failed to store: 100001` on stderr and exited 0, and the push that landed `b42b06d` printed the same line while reporting `* [new branch] directive-authoring-consolidation-gate -> directive-authoring-consolidation-gate`.
Consequence: An executor at the end of a session pushes, sees `fatal:` on stderr, and reads the region that governs that moment — Stop conditions — which says any failed command is a stop. The sentence that says the line is noise is in a region marked BASE VERIFICATION whose paragraph opens "Before anything else", so a reader scoping the sentence to its paragraph reads the widening as applying to the base fetch. The executor stops and reports a failure on a push that landed, which is the state the branch is then left in: the ref is on the remote, the session has reported it as unlanded, and the decision session triaging the report cannot tell which without checking the remote itself. The amendment's own directive had to hoist the sentence to its top level rather than rely on the region, which is evidence the region does not carry it far enough.
Fix: Two shapes, either within the editing constraints. Restate the sentence's scope so its widening is not read off its paragraph — "This applies to every remote operation this directive runs, at whatever point it runs" — or add the exception where the trigger is, in the Stop conditions region: a remote operation whose refs report success is not a failed command. The second is the smaller edit and puts the exception where it is read. Criterion 11.

Consequence: Two sessions reporting the same command honestly report different environments — one writes "worktree", one writes "sandbox" — and a decision session comparing two reports cannot tell whether a difference in the field means the counts were taken in different places or described on different axes, which is the whole of what the field was added to settle. Fix: Name one axis and make the values exhaust it. "with the tree it was observed in — the clone's main tree, or the worktree the directive assigns" decides the question the field was added for, and if whether a command was sandboxed also matters it is a second clause, not a third alternative in the same list. Criterion 11.
