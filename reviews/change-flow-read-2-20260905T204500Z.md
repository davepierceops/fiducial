## Skepticism pass

Verdict (skepticism pass): changes-required

**Where does a fix hide a review that will not happen?** Three places, in descending order of what it costs.

The first is Depth 3 (N-9). Cycle 1 did not raise it; revision 2 creates it. Line 95 moves the full-sweep continuity scan off the Spec Reviewer's on-demand list and onto intake, "on the rules store". `rules/` does not exist at d97e545 — verified by running. So the instrument that asks whether anything in the whole methodology contradicts anything else is, as of this revision, assigned to a process operating on a store that has not been built, and simultaneously removed from the one role that could run it today. R1137 is not dead text being tidied away; it is the only row in the corpus that covers methodology-wide contradiction, and this is the document that most needs it run, because it changes four standing rows across three files. The sentence reads as a tightening — "not on demand" — and its effect for the interval is that the sweep is nobody's.

The second is the verdict (N-2). The document now requires a verdict line per pass, and the schema it names in the same sentence admits one. That sounds clerical and is not: the verdict line is the artifact's only load-bearing output, the field the schema maps sign-off and the ship call onto, and a requirement that cannot be met in the artifact's own shape is met by the reviewer picking one. A reviewer that has run both passes and can emit one verdict will emit the one that reads as the overall call — which is the quality call, because it is the one written first and the one the word "verdict" most naturally attaches to. The skeptic pass's conclusion then survives only as prose, which is exactly the demotion R0492 exists to prevent. This artifact had to invent a workaround to comply with both; the next reviewer will invent a different one.

The third is the routine one-session case, still (N-8). Cycle 1 argued this at length and Dave ruled: the passes stay separate, the sessions merge for routine deltas. That ruling stands and is not reopened here. What is left unstated is narrower and mechanical: R0487 binds `reviewer-agent`, R0488 binds `skeptic-risk-agent`, and lines 84–85 put both on one reader without saying that a session may hold both roles — where R0486 in the same range makes role-distinctness load-bearing for the Coder and the Test Designer. The residual risk is not that one session is too few; it is that neither role document is addressed to the session actually doing the work.

**Does the second trigger buy what F-8 wanted?** Partly, and it should be bought knowingly. F-8's real complaint was that an unbounded internal refactor gets one reader. Line 88 fixes the reader count by letting the reader escalate — but the escalation is declared "in the verdict", which is to say after the read is finished. A reader who could not hold the delta whole has already produced the read that says so; the second session then arrives to review a delta the first reader has already characterised. That is a weaker instrument than a size test applied before the read, and it inherits the incentive F-4's exemption had: the party who would have to summon a second session is the one whose read would be superseded. Not a finding, because F-8's fix is what Dave ruled and it is a real improvement over none; recorded because the trigger's placement is doing less work than its wording suggests.

**What the label costs.** N-4 is filed as non-blocking on the reasoning that the second trigger is scoped to reader count. The skeptical reading is less comfortable: the word `consequential` in this corpus is attached to R0534, which requires Dave's explicit go before release. A document that lets a reader apply that word on its own judgment has, on a literal reading, given every reader a way to route a large internal refactor to Dave's desk. The failure is in the safe direction and that is why it is not blocking — but the scarce resource in this system is Dave's attention, and this is a new, ungated path to spending it.

## N-1 — blocking
Claim: The principle and the consequential bullet give opposite remedies for the same condition — a delta one reader cannot read whole.
Location: process/change-flow.md:24–26 against process/change-flow.md:86–90
Evidence: Inferred by reading, both sentences new in the diff. Line 24: "A rebuild is one delta too, if one reader can read it whole; if not, it is more than one, and the reader is the one who says so." Line 88: "or the reader finds the delta exceeds what one session can read whole and says so in the verdict, at which point a second session is summoned." The trigger is the same in both ("one reader can read it whole" / "exceeds what one session can read whole") and the declarer is the same in both ("the reader is the one who says so" / "says so in the verdict"). The consequences are not: one splits the delta, the other keeps one delta and adds a session. Both sentences were written to close F-8, whose fix asked for a size trigger and did not say which remedy it drives.
Consequence: An agent that reaches the condition cannot proceed. Splitting the delta and summoning a second session produce different artifacts, different closing commits, and different answers to what the reviewed range is — a split delta closes twice against two ranges, a two-session delta closes once against one. In the code case the two are not even both available: the delta is a branch landing as one pull request, so splitting it means re-cutting branches after a read has already run. The reader will pick whichever sentence it read last, and two readers of the same delta will disagree about what happened.
Fix: Rule which one, and state the other as its consequence rather than as an alternative. The reading that costs least: the size call summons a second session (line 88), and line 24's clause becomes advice about how to *plan* a delta rather than what to do when a read is already under way — "if one reader cannot read it whole, it should have been more than one" — or is struck, since the second trigger now carries the bound line 24 was restored to carry.
