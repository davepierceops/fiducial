## Skepticism

Verdict (skepticism): changes-required

**Where does "one read" hide a review that will not happen?** Three places, in descending order of what it costs.

The first is the convergence phase, and it is new in this revision (F-1, F-2). The document abolishes a status and then retires rows by whether they mention it. What mentions it is every rule that governs the interval those rows exist to police: the licence to write tests (R0016), the bar on implementing (R0017), the definition of what the Test Designer tests against (R1152), the requirement that the red-gate run before agreement (R0087), the requirement that the spec's suite be confirmed red on bad logic (R0478). Retire them by vocabulary and the interval the document calls "open" — the interval in which all spec work happens — is governed by this document's four sentences and nothing else. Add F-2 and the entry condition goes too: under DEC-000360 a spec reached the test-writing state only after a reviewer had read it and Dave had said so; under lines 147–154 it reaches that state by having a commit on a branch. So the reviews that will not happen are the first gate on a spec and the behavioural red-gate on its suite, and the document does not know it has removed them, because it states at line 175 that it retires DEC-000360's mechanism and not its substance. This is exactly the failure both prior cycles found most often — a row changed without being named — arriving this time through a clause written to fix it.

The second is the verdict (F-6). Revision 3 fixes cycle 2's N-2 properly: three verdict lines, one per pass plus an overall. What it does not say is how the overall is computed, and the routine case is one session writing all three. A reviewer whose quality pass is `ready` and whose skepticism pass is `changes-required` has no stated overall value and will pick one. The demotion N-2 warned about is narrower now — the skeptic conclusion survives in its own line and cannot be silently dropped — but the field the schema maps sign-off onto is still resolved by the reviewer's taste, and the safe direction is not the cheap one.

The third is the deep read (F-3). The instrument that asks whether anything in the methodology contradicts anything else is either the Spec Reviewer's standing obligation under R1137 or a discretionary call by Dave and the Chief of Staff, and the document says both. In the interval — which is now, because `rules/` does not exist — the difference is whether the sweep has an owner or a requester. Cycle 2 filed this as an observation about ordering; revision 3 stated the ordering and stated it twice, differently.

**Where does the one-session case let the skeptic pass become a formality?** Less than in any prior revision, and the improvement is real rather than verbal. Fixing the order (Reviewer first, then Skeptic), binding the session to both role documents' rows, and requiring the skeptic pass to emit its own verdict line together mean the second question must be answered on the page, in its own words, after the first. R1055's ban on empty reviews reaches the session through the role binding at line 92. What remains is structural and not fixable by drafting: the party writing the skeptic verdict has just written the quality verdict, and no rule says the two may differ or what follows if they do. F-6 is the tractable half of that.

**Is the size call something a reader will actually make?** Cheaper than cycle 2 assessed, and less useful. Cheaper because revision 3 summons the second session "for the skeptic pass" (line 99) rather than for a re-read: the first reader's quality pass is not superseded, so the reader who makes the call is no longer voting to discard its own work. That removes the incentive problem cycle 2 identified, and it is a genuine improvement nobody asked for. Less useful because the remedy does not address the condition. The trigger is "the delta exceeds what one session can read whole"; the remedy hands the same whole delta to a second session, which must read all of it to run the skeptic pass. Two sessions each read the delta whole; neither is relieved of the thing the call was made about. What the call actually buys is that the delta is read twice rather than once — valuable, but it is a depth remedy answering a size complaint, and the document's own sentence at line 30 concedes it ("the lesson for next time is that it should have been planned as more than one"). Filed as F-8, an accepted risk rather than a defect, because the trigger is Dave's ruling and the split-the-delta alternative was closed deliberately in closing N-1. It should be accepted knowing that the size call improves review depth and does nothing about review size.

**One thing this read did not find.** No sentence in revision 3 gives an agent two answers about what to do, which is what both prior cycles found and what makes a document unusable. The blocking findings here are of a different and quieter kind — the document is wrong about what it is changing, not internally inconsistent about what to do. That is the failure mode a closing-section list exists to catch, and it is the first revision to have one.
