## The ruling on `detail.branch_head`'s narrow scope, on the merits

Asked for independently of the decision record, and answered independently of it. **The narrow scope is correct.** Three questions, in the order the directive puts them.

**Is a report on FM-4 through FM-9 impoverished by its absence?** No, and the reason is structural rather than a judgment about how much detail a report should carry. The only use a session has for `<branch>`'s pre-invocation SHA is recovering a tip that step 6 would otherwise orphan. Step 5's second check runs **before** step 6 and refuses exactly when `<branch>` carries a commit the base does not (§3.2 step 5, §3.3). So on every path where step 6 completed — FM-5 through FM-9 and the success path — the check passed, which means `<branch>` was an ancestor of the base, which means its prior tip is an ancestor of the base and therefore of the new head. Nothing is orphaned and there is nothing to recover; the SHA would be a fact about a commit the session can still reach. On FM-4, step 6 is the step that failed, no ref moved, and `<branch>` still holds its own tip, which `git rev-parse <branch>` reads directly (*observed* by probe, recorded under delta (d)). So on all six failure modes and the success path, the key's value is either unreachable-by-construction or trivially available locally. That is the strongest form of "not impoverished": not that the omission is tolerable, but that the guard's ordering makes the fact useless there.

**Is the asymmetry with `detail.prior_branch` principled?** Yes, and it is the test that confirms the ruling rather than an exception to it. `prior_branch` *is* carried on FM-5 through FM-9 and the success path, and §5.3 line 848 gives the reason: it records a mutation of the session's own working tree that survives the invocation and that the session cannot otherwise recover from the report. `branch_head` records a pre-state that is either unchanged (FM-4) or an ancestor of what replaced it (FM-5 onward). Two keys, one carried wide and one narrow, on a stated and correct distinction.

**Is the table's condition well-formed and decidable on every path?** Not quite, and this is the one place the narrow scope needs work — **N1**. "The ref whose check refused" is decidable whenever exactly one check refuses, and it is what §6's FM-3 cell and AC-LAND-T03 both key off. It has no fixed referent when both would refuse, because §3.2 step 5 never says whether the second check is evaluated after the first has failed. The gap is narrow: no ref has moved at that point either, so nothing is lost by it, which is why it is N1 and not blocking.
