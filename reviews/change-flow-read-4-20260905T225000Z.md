The likely outcome is not that the wrong thing is done but that the read shrinks to whoever happened to look at the spec, with nothing recording that it happened — which is how a first gate becomes a formality, and DEC-000360's ordering existed precisely because a suite written against an unread spec encodes that spec's defects as tests. **Where does the verdict rule mislead?** Line 140–141 makes the overall call arithmetic — the most severe of the pass verdicts — which is the right answer to cycle 3's F-6 and removes the reviewer's taste from the field the corpus reads for sign-off. But the read has three dimensions (lines 63–85) and the document names only two of them passes (line 87). Continuity, the dimension that catches the failure this document keeps having, is the one with no verdict line and no term in the maximum. A read whose continuity dimension is changes-required and whose two passes are clean computes to `ready` under the rule as written. This artifact is the near-instance: continuity is changes-required, and the overall is changes-required only because skepticism independently reached the same value. Had the completeness claim not also been a lie, F-1 would have had no path into the ship call.

**Where does the one-session case still let the skeptic pass go quiet?** Unchanged from cycle 3, and unchanged for a structural reason revision 4 does not pretend to fix: the party writing the skeptic verdict has just written the quality verdict. What revision 4 adds is that the two verdicts now visibly differ or visibly agree, and the overall is computed rather than chosen, so a skeptic verdict more severe than the quality verdict can no longer be quietly absorbed into a milder overall. That is a real narrowing. This artifact demonstrates it: the two passes differ, and the rule, not the reviewer, decided.

**One thing this read did not find.** No sentence in revision 4 gives an agent two answers about what to do.
