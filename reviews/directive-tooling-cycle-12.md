## N1 — non-blocking
Claim: The fallback is dictated and stated as a binary on **manifest presence**, so the document's consequent claim — that M3 is decidable on *every* directive and that AC-DT-10 no longer reaches it — is false for a directive carrying a manifest that does not locate the disposition author region.
Location: specs/directive-tooling.md §4, the M3 row of the mechanically-checkable table (`specs/directive-tooling.md:676`); §4, "Two consequences, stated because a fallback not carried to the criteria is not a fallback" (`:741`); §6 AC-DT-06, the M3 clause (`:1544`).
Evidence: Verified by reading, at the revision under review. The M3 row reads "where the directive carries a G11 manifest the search runs over the disposition author region **as the manifest locates it**", and then "M3 is therefore decidable on **every** directive, and no directive makes it an element the lint cannot decide (AC-DT-10)" (*observed*). §4's consequences paragraph reads "there is no directive on which M3 has no extent to search" (*observed*). G11's own text requires that "every region is marked and the manifest enumerates them all" and that the manifest carry "exactly one classification per entry, never both and never neither" (`:601`, `:608`, *observed*) — but G11 is a property of what **`bin/directive` emits**, and the lint is handed a landed file with no guarantee that the manifest in it is the one the generator wrote. §4 says so itself two paragraphs earlier: "Extent is therefore computable by reading the landed file together with the manifest it carries, without knowing what the generator emitted" (*observed*) — which is exactly the case where the manifest may be present and incomplete. A manifest that is present but names no disposition author region satisfies neither branch of the binary: the first has no referent for "as the manifest locates it", and the second applies only "where it carries none".
Consequence: Concretely, take a generated skeleton whose manifest an author hand-edits, deleting the disposition author region's entry while leaving the prompt region's. M3 has no located extent, so it is an element the lint cannot decide, and AC-DT-10 requires it to be reported unknown and to exit non-zero. That is the **safe** outcome and it does not contradict AC-DT-06, none of whose fixtures is of this shape — so this is not the opposed-exit contradiction cycle 11's B1 filed, and it is not blocking. What is wrong is the universal: two sites now assert that no directive makes M3 undecidable and that AC-DT-10 no longer reaches M3, and a reader who takes either sentence as the contract will build a lint that has no branch for the case, or will read AC-DT-10 as dead text for M3 when it is not. A second, smaller consequence: §7's accepted-risk list names one M3 residual and is silent on this case, so the list is complete for the paste residual and silent for the incomplete-manifest one. Note this is a reading of the document, not an observation of a tool: neither binary exists and no manifest was inspected (see *Not inspected*).
Fix: Two options, and the choice is Dave's. **(a) Narrow the universal.** Keep the binary and qualify the consequent: M3 is decidable on every directive whose manifest, where present, locates the disposition author region; a manifest present but not locating it leaves M3 undecidable and AC-DT-10 governs. One clause at each of the three sites, and one sentence in §7. **(b) Re-key the fallback on locatability rather than on presence.** M3 runs region-scoped where the manifest **locates** the disposition author region, and whole-file otherwise — which restores the universal honestly and needs no AC-DT-10 carve-out, at the cost of extending the whole-file search to a file that does carry a prompt region, where the O4 residual then bites for real rather than by deliberate paste. Option (a) is the smaller and is the one this reviewer would propose, **clearly labelled as a proposal**; neither is taken here, because the sentence at issue is the one the dictated disposition installs, and re-wording it would be the reinterpretation the directive's stop conditions forbid.
Related: O4.
