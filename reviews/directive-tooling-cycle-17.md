## B1 — blocking
Claim: Nothing in the document bounds the disposition **prompt** from carrying the label's lexical form, so a generator conforming to AC-DT-03 can emit a skeleton carrying **two** labelled disposition statements, which fails M3 — the same "J1's output fails J2" defect the cycle-16 gate filed as blocking, reintroduced by the fix for it.
Location: specs/directive-tooling.md:1792–:1803 (AC-DT-03, the prompt region's enumeration); :663–:676 (§4 G3, the prompt bullet); :893 (§4, "A prompt is not a labelled disposition statement"); :845 (§4, M3's row, the exactly-one count); :1940 (AC-DT-06 fixture (iv)).
Evidence: **Verified by reading**, at the reviewed commit, and **by running** for the site sweep. Every site that excludes the prompt from M3's match does so **categorically** — "a prompt is not a labelled disposition statement, so it is outside M3's match whatever it quotes" (`:893`, *observed*) — and that claim was safe under the previous revision, where the label existed nowhere in the skeleton by construction and the prompt named only the two admitted forms. This cycle's B1 changes the premise: the generator now emits the label as a fixed lexical string (`:677-700`, AC-DT-03 `:1804-1808`). AC-DT-03 fixes the prompt's content **by enumeration** — read from committed text (G1), states that a disposition is required, names both admitted forms, states that the disposition is written as its own labelled statement — and the enumeration is not closed: no clause says the prompt states *only* those things, and the prompt's text is whatever its committed source says, a source Q1 leaves undetermined. `grep -n "prompt" specs/directive-tooling.md | grep -i label` returns ten sites and **none** requires the prompt's source text to omit the label's form or to carry it fenced (*observed*, by running). The exclusion at `:893` is stated as a property of what a prompt *is*, not of what its text *contains*, and M3 matches text.
Consequence: Concrete, and on the main journey. Q1 resolves to a committed source that explains the working-tree requirement; the natural way to explain "write the disposition as its own labelled statement" is to show the label. The generator reads that text into the prompt region (G1, AC-DT-02), emits the label again into the author region (AC-DT-03), and every skeleton it produces carries **two** unfenced labelled disposition statements. M3's count clause fails all of them at J2 — the executor's first act — so `bin/directive`'s ordinary output fails `bin/check-directive`, which is exactly the defect the cycle-16 gate's B1 was blocking for and exactly what this cycle's B1 disposition set out to prevent. The document also cannot be built to as written: an implementer whose invariant source shows the label satisfies §6 and ships the failure; one whose source does not satisfies §6 and ships a working pair. Two conforming implementations, differing in whether the tool works — the shape cycle 8's B2, cycle 13's B1, and cycle 16's B1 were each blocking for. Note what is **not** wrong: the emitted label itself is right, and the property the document now states — that a faithfully filled skeleton passes M3 — is the right property. What is missing is the condition that makes the property true, now that a second occurrence of the label is possible.
Fix: One clause, and the fence exclusion this cycle already adopted supplies the mechanism. Add to AC-DT-03's prompt-region enumeration that the prompt carries **no unfenced labelled disposition statement** — it may show the label only inside a fenced block, which M3 does not match (§4, "The count is exactly one") — and state the same bound at G3's prompt bullet so the categorical claim at `:893` is not read as covering it. Equivalently and more strongly, state as a clause of G3 or as a goal the property the whole apparatus exists to secure: **a generated skeleton carries exactly one unfenced labelled disposition statement, and filling its content slot faithfully passes M3**. That property is asserted at J1, J2, and §4 as a consequence; it is not stated anywhere as a requirement a generator must meet, which is why nothing catches the prompt.
Related: N2.

## N1 — non-blocking
Claim: M3's **exactly-one** count is still a requirement no governed file states; the revision discloses it rather than closing it, and the disclosure makes AC-DT-13 a criterion with a named exception, which is a weaker thing than the criterion it replaces.
Location: specs/directive-tooling.md:845 (§4, M3's row, the Decision qualification cell); :940–:975 (§4, "The count is exactly one"); :2108–:2125 (AC-DT-13, the exception clause); :2494–:2510 (§7, "Not accepted", "**Four** elements … one stands now").
Evidence: **Verified by reading**, both texts at their commits, and by running for the governed source. The governed rule at `skills/directive-authoring.md` @ `b4a0fa58` `:15-20` reads whole: "Every directive states its working-tree disposition — either an exclusive assignment (a named directory plus the command creating it) or an explicit sole-tree declaration. A prohibition is not a disposition. The disposition is stated as its own labelled statement, mechanically distinguishable from incidental mention of trees or commands elsewhere in the file; the label's fixed form is a tooling concern, not this document's." (*observed*, read at the commit). Label-presence, form-membership, and at-least-one read off it; **no count does**. The document now says so in its own words — "the sentence states no count, and its only carrier is the definite singular of 'the disposition' … a reading, not a read-off" (`:962-970`, *observed*) — and records the exposure at §7 and AC-DT-13. That is an honest execution of the directive's "Ground, stated", and it is the cycle-16 gate's option (c) in substance; the directive dictated neither (a) nor (b) nor (c) by name, so this is the author-session's reading of what "keep exactly-one and state the ground" obliges, and it should be checked as such.
Consequence: Two, and both are Dave's rather than the author's. **First**, AC-DT-13 asserted "Every element M1–M8 satisfies this as written" and now asserts it "for every requirement it carries but one." The criterion stays testable — the exception is named, not vague — but what it certifies is narrower, and a release gate reading §6 as a checklist now passes a document that ships a stated G6 violation rather than none. **Second**, the fence exclusion is bound to the count: it exists to make exactly-one survivable for directives that originate other directives' wording. Take option (a) — narrow M3 to at-least-one — and the count, the fence exclusion, residual two, and this exposure all go together, which is a materially smaller M3 than the one specified here. That coupling is stated nowhere in the document and is worth seeing before choosing.
Fix: **Clearly labelled as a proposal; the choice is Dave's and none is taken here.** The cycle-16 gate's three options stand unchanged and this reviewer narrows none of them — **(a)** narrow M3 to at-least-one, deleting the count, the fence exclusion, and residual two together; **(b)** keep exactly-one and source it by expedited amendment to `skills/directive-authoring.md`, the mechanism this effort has used four times; **(c)** keep exactly-one and carry the disclosure, which is what this revision does. If (c) stands, one addition would make it auditable: state at §4 or §7 that the fence exclusion is **dependent** on the count, so a later cycle taking (a) knows what else retires with it.
Related: B1.

## N2 — non-blocking
Claim: No criterion requires the generator and the lint to take the label from **one** source, so the "by construction" property the document now rests on is secured only by AC-DT-06 fixture (iv) — which §8 Q9 defers until the TRD fixes the label form.
Location: specs/directive-tooling.md:347 (J2, "the generator and the lint cannot disagree about the label"); :916–:938 (§4, "The two generated cases"); :1940 (AC-DT-06 fixture (iv)); :1881 (AC-DT-06, the fixtures-are-specified-now clause); :2649–:2668 (§8 Q9); :1690 (AC-DT-02).
Evidence: **Verified by reading**, at the reviewed commit. J2 states that the two tools "cannot disagree about the label, because one of them fixes it and the other matches what it emitted" (*observed*), and §4 states the passing-by-construction property twice. AC-DT-02 checks the generator's **invariant** regions against the committed sources its manifest names; the label is explicitly **not** invariant text — G3 states that G1 does not reach it, the governed rule having delegated its form to tooling (`:687-694`, *observed*) — so AC-DT-02 does not reach it either. The only criterion that would catch a generator and a lint holding different label constants is AC-DT-06(iv), whose exit-0 verdict fails if they differ. And AC-DT-06 now says its M3 fixtures are "specified now" and "instantiable as files once the TRD fixes the label's lexical form" (*observed*), with Q9 naming the TRD as what resolves it.
Consequence: Bounded but real. Between this revision and the TRD, the document's central new property has **no checkable statement**: it is asserted in three places as a consequence and tested in one place that cannot yet be run. That is not a false claim — the property does follow, given one label constant — but the document nowhere requires one label constant, and a TRD is free to give the generator and the lint separate ones without contradicting §6. This is the scheduling half of B1: the same undecided form is what B1 shows the prompt may duplicate, and neither the duplication nor the divergence has a criterion.
Fix: One clause at AC-DT-03 or as a PRD-level constraint on the TRD: the label's lexical form is fixed **once**, in one place both tools read, and the generator emits exactly what M3 matches. Q9 is the natural home for the second half — it already names the TRD as what resolves the form, and naming "one source for both tools" as part of what the TRD must fix costs a sentence and removes the divergence from the accepted-unknown pile.
