## B1 — blocking

No union type is introduced", and that on FM-1 through FM-6 `files` is `[]`. §3.7's rule produces exactly that rejected leaf form on those six paths, because `files` is absent from `facts` when no commit was made. For `verification`: §3.7 enumerates step 10 as returning "the per-file results" and names no step that establishes `verification`, so on FM-7 — where step 9's push failed and step 10 never ran — `verification` is absent from `facts` and §3.7's rule emits `{"value": null, "class": "unknown"}`, while §6's FM-7 row (unchanged since `47758d14`) states "`verification` is `incomplete`". §3.7 asserts of this rule that it "makes B4's class of defect structurally hard to reintroduce".
Consequence: Two implementers produce two different reports on seven of the nine detected failure modes, and the divergence is not recoverable by reading more carefully — the two sections state opposite things. On `files` it is worse than a disagreement: an implementation built to §3.7 emits a union type into the parse contract, and AC-LAND-T01, the criterion this cycle strengthened, then fails it, because T01 asserts "the three value-domain rules of §5.2 hold" and §5.2's `files` rule is "`files` is a list, possibly empty". ## The four items the revision's executor flagged

**§3.7's placement.** Sound, and the reason given is the right one. `specs/trd-template.md` §3 requires "Interfaces between components" as a bullet under §3 and prescribes no ordering among subsections, so placing them at §3.7 rather than as a new §3.2 satisfies the template exactly. The reason — that §3.2's step numbering is cited by number throughout this document and across `reviews/bin-land-trd-cycle-1.md`, and renumbering would strand every citation — is a real cost avoided, and §3.1 forward-references the section so a reader following the components table arrives at the seams. Nothing turns on it. No finding.

**AC-LAND-T01a as a new criterion.** Correctly scoped and correctly argued. §5.4 declares its contents "TRD-stage criteria ... proposed for the derived acceptance-criteria artifact and are not an amendment to PRD §6", and T01a stays inside that: it asserts a property of the serialization contract, which is precisely what AC-LAND-07 defers to this document. The stated reason for splitting it from T01 — "a criterion asserting both shapes at once would be satisfied by either" — is the correct reason, and it is the same reason T01's own enumeration of failure modes is what makes T01 a real test. It is consistent with §3.2 step 11's usage-error bullet and with §7's mapping of 2, and it does not collide with AC-X-3's requirement that `--help` exit 0, since `--help` is not an invocation `argparse` rejects. Asserting that stderr is non-empty rather than asserting its wording keeps it inside `bin/tests/helpers.py`'s standing rule that tests assert on codes, never on English wording. No finding. It also sets the precedent N1's fix should follow.

**The exact-ref-selection extension at §3.2 step 3.** Load-bearing and correct, and it is the part of the D-N1 collapse that makes the collapse safe. Selecting by exact ref name rather than by position is what stops a second matching ref from silently supplying the base. Both of the extension's *observed* claims were re-run against `git` 2.55.0 over a `file://` remote and both held: with `sub/main` present at the remote, `git ls-remote --heads origin feat main` returns three lines for two patterns, `refs/heads/sub/main` among them (*observed*); and `git ls-remote --heads origin nosuchbranch` prints nothing and exits 0 (*observed*). The second is what makes branch-absence a readable result rather than a failure, and the document says so. This is the executor going past what D-N1 asked for and getting it right. N2 is the one state the extension does not cover — an empty result on *both* patterns — and it is a gap in step 3 rather than a defect in this extension.

**The step-4 refuse-without-asserted-cause extension.** The best-judged of the four. D-O2 asked only that step 4's premise carry a provenance class; the executor labelled it *inferred*, said why it was not run ("demonstrating it needs a concurrent writer"), then went and found a cause it *could* run and reported that the refusal is identical either way. The narrowed-refspec claim was re-run and held: with `remote.origin.fetch` restricted to `main` and a branch created at the remote afterwards, `ls-remote` reports that branch's head while `git cat-file -e <sha>^{commit}` fails with "Not a valid object name" and exits 128 (*observed*, `git` 2.55.0 over a `file://` remote). The conclusion drawn — that the tool reports the state it observed and names no cause, because a report carrying only *observed* and *unknown* cannot honestly do otherwise — is the right conclusion and it is consistent with PRD G6. §6's FM-2 row was updated to match. No finding. This is the one place the document is stronger than the disposition required.
