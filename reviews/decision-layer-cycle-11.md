## DL-1 — blocking
Claim: Rule 13 states that a baton is never committed and never a file; Core defines a baton as an artifact, requires anything that must survive the session to be written down, and forbids chat as the sole record — and the repository holds three committed baton files, the most recent written the day rule 13 landed.
Location: docs/global-context/decision-layer.md:32 (rule 13, "**A baton is one paste block handed in chat:** nothing committed, no file, no execution session"), against docs/global-context/core.md:17 (Standing 4, "Artifacts are the record. Anything that must survive the session is written down. Chat is never the sole record of a decision.") and core.md:55 (Vocabulary, "**Baton** — the artifact a decision session hands its successor decision session: the package of unfinished responsibility — state, open questions, decisions in flight — that lets the receiver continue without the conversation that produced it.")
Evidence: Verified by running. `git diff 12ecaeb HEAD -- docs/global-context/decision-layer.md` shows rule 13 as the sole addition at 6211312, dated after all three of `docs/batons/baton-20260822T153848.md`, `docs/batons/baton-20260822T212629.md`, and `docs/batons/baton-20260823T003000.md`, which `ls docs/batons/` returns and which are tracked. core.md:10 states the collision rule for this exact case — "Layers loaded after it add rules; a rule in a layer that conflicts with a rule here does not waive it" — so the conflict resolves against rule 13 and the rule is unenforceable as written in the only bundle that carries it. Two further texts presuppose the losing side: roles/context-quality-reviewer.md:15-17 excludes "batons" from a scope whose base is the frontmatter in-scope set, an exclusion with nothing to exclude if batons are not files; and core.md:33 (Acting 14) prescribes the `<descriptor>-<timestamp>` filename form that all three baton files follow. `grep -rni baton decisions/` returns nothing, so no decision-log entry governs the point either way. Criterion 4 — the rule restates a term Core defines, and restates it differently. Surfaced, not resolved, per core.md:25 (Evidence 9).
Consequence: A decision session ending a session reads rule 13, hands its successor a chat paste block, and commits nothing. The successor holds the only copy of the state, open questions and decisions in flight in a conversation Core says may not be their sole record; if that chat is lost or not carried forward, the handoff is gone, which is the failure the baton exists to prevent. In the other direction, the three baton files already in the repository become records of a practice the governing text now forbids, and the reviewer role's scope carries a dead exclusion. A session that reads both files has no textual basis for choosing, and the choice determines whether the artifact survives.
Fix: One of the two texts changes, and which is Dave's. Either rule 13 drops "nothing committed, no file" and states the delivery form the three existing batons already follow — committed to `docs/batons/`, one paste block, SHA reported, as docs/batons/baton-20260822T212629.md:38 records deciding — or core.md:55 and core.md:17 are amended to carve the baton out of Standing 4 and to stop calling it an artifact. Not the reviewer's call, and the reviewer should not have to infer it.

## DL-2 — non-blocking
Claim: Rule 12's trigger is a condition the agent resolves entirely by its own reading of the session, on the one artifact that would record the session's own failures.
Location: docs/global-context/decision-layer.md:31 (rule 12, "**End non-trivial sessions with a retro.**")
Evidence: Verified by running. `grep -rn "non-trivial"` over all 51 in-scope files returns exactly one hit, this line — the term is defined nowhere in this file, in core.md, in LEXICON.md, or in skills/conversation-retro.md. The rule states no threshold, no examples, and no default; the sentence that follows it governs the retro's shape ("Evidence separate from interpretation; near-empty is a valid result"), not its trigger. Criterion 11 — "Underspecified conditions, missing escalation triggers."
Consequence: The obligation is discharged or skipped on the agent's own assessment of the session it just ran, and the assessment is unreviewable because a session that judged itself trivial leaves no artifact saying so. The bias runs one way: a session that went badly has the strongest reason to read itself as trivial. Rule 12's own second clause anticipates this — "near-empty is a valid result" — which only helps if the retro is written.
Fix: Make the default the other way and give the exception a test, in the rule's own idiom: "End every session with a retro; near-empty is a valid result. A session that produced no artifact and made no decision may skip it."

## DL-3 — non-blocking

The three conditions are stated at three different altitudes — a landmine, work that needs more than was asked, and an instruction that cannot be executed — and they overlap on the ordinary case where doing what was asked will produce a bad outcome. Criterion 11 — an underspecified condition the agent resolves by inference.
