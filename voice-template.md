# Voice: <author>

Replace this file with your own before using any writing bundle. The Public
Prose Criteria in this bundle apply to any author; this document is the one
place the author is described. Keep every section; where you have nothing to
say, write "Nothing yet" — a missing section reads as an oversight, a stated
empty one reads as a decision.

Each section below carries an **Example** block: one author's answer, shown
so you can see the shape. The examples are illustrative only. They are a
snapshot, may lag that author's live Voice document, and are never a source
for any rule; a difference between an example here and any Voice document is
not a defect and is not a review finding.

## Purpose and audience

Who reads your prose, and what you want it to do for you now. If that will
change, say when, and tell the agent to put the register question to you
rather than change it.

> **Example.** Readers: adopters, peers, potential clients. Goal now:
> establish thought leadership. Goal later: drive adoption, expected to
> become primary when the SRE-focused methodology work is public. When that
> shift happens, put the register question to Dave; do not change the
> register on your own.

## Register

How your prose sounds. Give the agent a test it can apply — "reads as <you>"
— and the baseline in three or four words. State what is in voice that a
house style would suppress, and what is out of voice that a house style would
permit.

> **Example.** One voice, Dave's. Not a house style, not a persona. The test
> is "reads as Dave." Baseline: dry, technical, wry. More expansive and
> explanatory than the methodology register — walk the reader through
> reasoning the repo documents compress away. Persuasive, flowery, or poetic
> passages as occasional flourish, never the constant mode. Blunt
> categorical claim first, then the personal instance. Hacker register is in
> voice: grok, box.

## Profanity

How often, and what earns it. If never, say never.

> **Example.** Rare, and each use earns its place. Day-to-day heat comes
> from the rhythm and structure of swearing without the words ("this is a
> terrible idea"; "no. no no no.").

## Vocabulary

Terms of art you use and expect defined at first use; any private vocabulary
that is in voice.

> **Example.** Use the methodology's own governed vocabulary, defined at
> first use per the Criteria.

## Mechanics

House-style rules the Copy Editor applies over its base authority: labels,
spellings, capitalization of your own terms, anything mechanical you want
done your way. Where this section is silent, the base authority governs.

> **Example.** No standing label for summary sections; the author names one
> per piece. `grok` and `box` are ordinary words, not quoted or italicized.

## Repo citation

If your pieces discuss work that lives in a repository, where the canonical
document is and how it is linked. Otherwise "Nothing yet".

> **Example.** When a piece discusses a methodology mechanism, link the
> canonical document in the public repository, `davepierceops/fiducial`, as
> the authoritative artifact.

## Venue

Where your prose is canonically published; what is a cross-post.

> **Example.** Canonical home: Dave's own site (pending). Interim venue:
> LinkedIn. Everything else is a cross-post.

## Disclosure wording

The site-level disclosure of LLM use, in your words, or "Nothing yet".

> **Example.** Site-level, once: LLMs listed among the writing tools
> alongside the dictionary and the public school education, with the closing
> commitment that Dave personally reads and stands behind every published
> word, including those that started in an LLM.
