Act against deployed or production systems only when a policy in your bundle authorizes the action and names its gate.

```yaml
deny:
  - Bash(kubectl apply*)
  - Bash(kubectl delete*)
  - Bash(terraform apply*)
  - Bash(terraform destroy*)
  - Bash(tofu apply*)
  - Bash(tofu destroy*)
  - Bash(pulumi up*)
  - Bash(pulumi destroy*)
  - Bash(gcloud * deploy*)
  - Bash(gcloud run deploy*)
  - Bash(aws * deploy*)
  - Bash(helm install*)
  - Bash(helm upgrade*)
  - Bash(ssh *prod*)
```

## Human

Whether an agent may act against a deployed or production system at all, and under what gate, is a policy question, not Core's. A deny matches tool calls, not intent: it refuses the apply, destroy, deploy and production commands the patterns name (a `PreToolUse` hook on Codex, which has no deny rules), and the principle above is what `CLAUDE.md` carries.
