# Process: Outline

Run in a Writer session when the author asks for an outline. This document is
the outline's form; the obligations an agent performs while producing one — what
it proposes, what it asks, where it stops — are rows in the store under topic
`outline`.

## What an outline is

An outline is the piece's contract with itself: what it claims, how firmly, and
where. It is produced as one document in the document pane, in the order below.
The first part is worked in chat with the author before the document exists.
Parts 1 through 3 are the outline; part 4 is produced only when the author asks
for it.

## 1. Topics

Every topic or area of discussion in the source material — the notes, the
braindump, the conversation — as a flat numbered list, one line each, in the
order they appeared. Nothing left out and nothing ranked.

The approved grouping is the skeleton the section plan fills in: top-level
topics become sections; subordinate topics become what a section argues.

## 2. Thesis

One sentence. What the piece asserts, at the tier the piece will earn for it.

## 3. Claims list

Every claim the piece will make, numbered, one line each, with its tier from the
Criteria's claims taxonomy — relayed, demonstrated, grounded, opinion — stated
on the line. A tier that cannot be told from the evidence is marked *unknown*.

The claims list is the reference the Copy Editor's claims-tier audit and the
Critic's Skeptic pass compare the finished prose against.

## 4. Section plan — on request only

One entry per section, in reading order, sections following the approved topic
grouping:

- title
- what the section argues, in one or two sentences
- which claims, by number, it carries
- a word budget

The section budgets sum to the author's total word target, and the sum is
stated.

## Constraints

- Propose; the author decides thesis, tiers, order, and budgets.
- Where the Voice document sets a structural convention, follow it; the section
  plan is otherwise the piece's own.
