# Public Prose Criteria

Read by the Writer, the Copy Editor, and the Critic before any work on a
piece. These criteria hold for any author's public prose under this method;
the author is described by the Voice document in this bundle, and where the
two speak to the same thing, apply the Voice document.

## Trust model

Assume the author reads every word of a piece before it publishes. Every
review pass is advisory; nothing a pass emits gates the piece.

## Readers with many languages

Write so that comprehension never depends on native-English-only cues. Quote
speech rather than introducing it with a bare colon. Make every line carrying
irony, wordplay, or idiom read correctly for a reader who takes it literally;
the play is bonus. Never simplify the register to achieve this.

## Claims taxonomy

Place every claim in a piece in one of four tiers, and make the tier legible
to the reader in the sentence itself:

1. **Relayed** — someone else's claim, passed along. Attribute it.
2. **Demonstrated** — the author's evidence constitutes proof: hypothesis,
   test, result. Show the work, or make clear the piece could.
3. **Grounded** — resting on experience, observation, or data below the bar
   for proof. Carry the tier in verb choice and frame: "what I noticed," "in
   my experience."
4. **Opinion** — clearest of all, by the same means.

This ladder is claim strength — how firmly a sentence may assert — not
provenance; state provenance per Core.

Treat tier-blurring — an opinion dressed as grounded, a grounded claim dressed
as proof — as a defect. Carry a claim's tier by verb and frame, never by
narrated self-correction: keep "what I noticed"; cut "I should be careful with
my own story here."

## Terminology

Define each governed or specialist term where it first appears for a public
reader. When an industry-standard term exists for a concept the piece names
differently, flag the mismatch to the author; the author decides which the
piece uses.

## Naming

Anonymize by default, in both directions: name criticized practices, not the
companies, products, or people; be as sparing with names in praise. Name a
party only where there is a clear benefit in that instance, and state the
benefit when proposing it.

Attribute always. When the author of a piece of work — an idea, a technique,
a phrase, a finding — is known, credit them, unless the work is trivial.
Anonymize-by-default governs targets and examples; it never licenses taking
credit by omission.

## Structure and length

Take length and structure from the author for the piece at hand; apply no
standing shape.

Wordy means duplicated ideas, not duplicated words; a draft inside its word
budget can still be wordy. When the author suspects duplication, report an
audit of the repeated ideas before cutting anything. The author sets a word
target; the writer chooses what goes. After a wordy draft, target 10% under
budget, not budget.

Make a point once, flat. Cut any contrast pair that restates it from the other
side. Cut any clause that explains the move it sits in. Cut scene-setting
clauses ("when I sit down to build," "from where I sit"); stance carries on
the verb.

## Continuity

Build across pieces is allowed; land every piece clean for a cold reader. An
explicitly declared series ("part 2 of N," stated up front) may assume its
predecessors.

## Discoverability

Prefer descriptive, searchable titles over clever ones; place key terms early;
keep the structure skimmable. Where discoverability and readability conflict,
readability wins. Never alter the register for discoverability.

## Venue

Write venue-independent prose with no platform-specific formatting
dependencies. The canonical home and cross-post venues are the Voice
document's.

## Disclosure

Disclose the use of LLMs once, at the site level, in register — never per
piece — with the author's commitment that they read and stand behind every
published word. The wording is the Voice document's.

## AI prose-smell — defect class

Treat each of the following as a defect wherever it appears. The list is
open: propose any new tell to the author; do not add one yourself.

- "load-bearing"
- em-dash cascades (a pair landing a payoff word is not a cascade)
- "it's not X, it's Y" constructions
- triadic sentence rhythm as default cadence
- hedge-then-assert patterns
- restating the thesis at every section opening
- summary sentences that add nothing ("This matters because...")
