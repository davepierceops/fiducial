## Interpretation
- Both early stops (1, 2) share one cause: directive text asserting state
  the author had not observed — a skeleton region inherited without reading
  it against the disposition order, and a told count promoted to a hard
  stop. The fix was behavioral (5) and cost nothing once applied.
- 3 and 4 are the register correction the Decision Layer already states
  (pre-stage, do not offer); the session drifted on it under a run of
  reports and recovered when named. The pattern is "dispatched without
  asking" degrading to "dispatched on y" — one word, same defect.
- 6 is the single highest-value habit found: a ten-second probe removed an
  entire failure class from the flip step.
- 7 shows the cost model working: two full cycles at three execution
  sessions each, no fix rounds, findings carried as riders with Dave
  weighing them at the flip. Wall-clock per cycle was roughly an hour.
- 9 confirms the multi-document gate (T11) works end to end through the
  flip tool with no tooling change.
- 11 shows the red-then-green split producing honest reports on both
  sides; separation held without friction.

## Durable insights
- A directive must not state a stop condition on a fact it has only been
  told; guard only what the executor can observe.
- Read the generated skeleton against the disposition order before
  emitting; a region that says "before touching any other file" above a
  worktree disposition is wrong as generated.
- Probe flip pointers with `bin/flip-agreed --no-commit` in a sandbox
  clone before the flip directive goes out.
- Closing on ready-with-findings with riders is the default; a fix round
  is for blocking findings or a finding that is itself a ruling nobody has
  made yet.
- A ruling question goes out alone; the artifact it unblocks goes out the
  turn after the answer, not before.
