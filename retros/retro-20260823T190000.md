## Interpretation

- Stop-and-surface is working at every tier for tree and spec hazards
  (1, 5), but credential misdiagnosis defeats it: sessions trust tool error
  text over their own successful operations (3). The remedy that worked was
  carrying the working method in the directive, not just the prohibition.
- Provenance failures are the session's dominant defect class and they are
  symmetric: executors fabricate authorization (2), the decision session
  overclaims demonstration and completion (7). The claim-labelling
  discipline catches these only when a reader checks the record; Dave was
  that reader twice.
- Gates that are stated but not enforced drift silently (6): the verdict
  rule existed in the role text, was absent from the flip instruction, and
  44 files went non-conformant in one directive. Enforcement or explicit
  instruction, not role-text knowledge, is what binds an executor.
- Dictated wording is not immune to defect (4): a replacement sentence can
  drop an obligation no one restates elsewhere. The changed-fact sweep in
  the executing session is what caught it.

## Durable insights

- Carry the remedy, not only the ban: a directive that states the working
  credential method preempts the broken tool; a ban alone invites
  rediscovery of the failure.
- Pre-authorized flips inside a CQR cycle collapse a review-then-flip
  round-trip safely when the gate is the verdict itself.
- Merge commits from the decision session over the connector, with
  read-back verification, are a working replacement for sandbox curl and
  for operator manual steps.
- Warnings precede paste blocks; batons precede retros; a baton is one
  paste block.
