## Interpretation

- The read-then-rule loop works best when the human's question is the unit
  (3, 13, 14). My multi-item triage bullets were the session's weakest
  artifact; one item per turn produced better rulings and reversed two of
  mine (14).
- I make two kinds of error the human catches reliably: stating an
  inference as fact (9), and designing backward from cost instead of forward
  from the right shape (11). Both are habits, not slips, and both were named
  as such.
- Rules that are loaded are not necessarily applied (12). The rule I failed
  to apply was mid-bundle prose; the replacement the human wrote is seven
  words at the top of core. That is the same argument the process sweep was
  making, demonstrated on me.
- "What would an agent do wrong without this?" is now the working test for
  every candidate rule (13), and it drops more than it keeps.
- The executor stops this session (6, 17) were both my directive errors;
  both executors stopped correctly under the stop conditions. The
  methodology's cost for a bad directive is one round-trip, which is the
  right cost.
- Command blocks are trusted in proportion to how they were tested (10).
  End-to-end against a stand-in remote, including the guard and re-run
  paths, is the bar; I had been meeting a lower one.

## Durable insights

- A bundle's order is a ruling about the whole; it belongs in one place
  (the topic sequence), not distributed across rows. The row's `order` is
  only ever its position within its topic.
- For a one-user store, migration cost is near zero and will never be again;
  design the right shape now and pay the migration.
- "Main is always deployable" (releasable) subsumes every merge gate as a
  property rather than a mechanism; the mechanism-form rule ("never merge
  unreviewed…") is the one that gets reasoned around.
- Sed-class changes go in a tested command block from the human's clone, not
  a directive; a change that needs per-sentence judgment (pronoun rewrites)
  goes to a session. The line between them is "does the tool need to
  reason?"
- A "hollow" background-agent completion (zero tool calls, seconds) is a
  recognizable failure; the check is the tool-use count, not the prose.
