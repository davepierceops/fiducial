# Retro — fiducial — 2026-08-27

## Context

A Chief of Staff decision session opened on the post-cleanup baton and was
directed toward the bin/directive + bin/check-directive TRD. Before the TRD
directive went out, Dave asked who had written the directive-tooling PRD, read
it in the artifact pane, ruled it unreadable, and redirected the session:
rewrite the PRD to substance only. The session produced a 511-line rewrite of
the 3,403-line agreed document, landed it on a branch over the connector,
opened PR #225, ran the cycle-21 Spec Reviewer gate, triaged its findings with
Dave, and dispatched the cycle-22 fix directive twice — the first dispatch
stopped correctly on a worktree conflict the directive had not anticipated.

## Evidence

1. Dave did not recognize the PRD as something he had made: "I don't remember
   ever writing a PRD for this." The origin (executor-authored from his
   dictated directive, 20 cycles of his per-finding rulings) was recoverable
   only by reading the cycle-1 directive file.
2. The agreed PRD carried a ~130-line preamble indexing 19 cycle directives by
   SHA, per-sentence provenance tags, and changelog prose of the form
   "previously read X; the cycle-N gate found Y; it now reads Z" through every
   section. §4 ran ~1,500 lines, most of it reviewer-driven justification.
3. Dave's ruling on seeing it rendered: "get rid of all the bullshit... I bet
   it'll be less than a thousand lines." The substance-only rewrite came to
   511 lines (+441/−3,301 on the diff), keeping all goals, the M1–M8 table,
   AC-DT-01–19 under their identifiers, risks, and the seven open questions.
4. The 3,403-line document was unreadable in the artifact pane as raw
   markdown; a rendered HTML copy with a contents sidebar is what Dave
   actually reviewed, and the review took minutes once it existed.
5. The cycle-21 gate on the rewrite returned changes-required: 4 blocking,
   5 non-blocking, 4 observations — twelve of thirteen were loss-check
   findings (substance cut along with the bloat: §7's risk sets, the
   no-retrofit disposition, six of eight M8 fixtures, §6's preamble). One
   finding (O1, audience narrowed) was rejected on observation: the prior
   text already read `audience: [human]`.
6. The first cycle-22 fix directive failed at the worktree add: the finished
   gate session's worktree still held the local branch. The executor stopped
   exactly per the directive's stop wording, reported three routes, and took
   none. The directive had verified the remote branch state but not local
   branch/worktree holders.
7. The rewrite landed over the connector as a single ~31KB file write, one
   commit with the decision record, verified by get_commit stats. The
   pre-commit hook did not run, which was correct: the intended frontmatter
   (status: draft) landed as written.
8. Dave's turns for the whole arc after the ruling: "ship", "y", "go", "y",
   "baton", "retro".

## Interpretation

- The bloat is process-caused, not author-caused (1, 2). Twenty cycles of
  reviewers filing findings and executors answering them inside the document
  converts a spec into a transcript of its own review. Any long cycle chain
  under the current process will reproduce this. (Candidate change below.)
- A rewrite that strips process residue will also cut substance, and the
  existing gate catches it well (3, 5): the loss-check instruction — diff
  against the prior agreed text, report absent substance as findings, with the
  ruling's removal categories explicitly out of scope — turned a risky
  wholesale rewrite into a bounded two-cycle operation.
- Document review throughput is render-limited (4). Dave rejected the same
  content twice in raw form and ruled on it in minutes once rendered with
  navigation. The methodology has no stated form for "human reads a long
  governed document"; the artifact-pane-plus-chat pattern assumes documents of
  reviewable length.
- Directive worktree preconditions must be checked against local state, not
  just origin (6). Remote-state verification is necessary but not sufficient
  when a branch is reused across sessions; the stale-worktree case is now a
  known class with a working remedy (remove without --force, stop on failure,
  check out the existing branch without -b).
- The connector is the right transport for a decision-session-authored
  single-file landing (7): fast, verifiable by stats, and its hook bypass is a
  feature when the lifecycle state is deliberate.
- The stop-condition wording continues to earn its two lines (6): second
  correct executor stop in two days, each with a triageable report and zero
  self-recovery.

## Durable insights

- Governed documents should carry current substance only; finding
  dispositions, provenance, and change narrative belong in review artifacts
  and cycle directives, which already exist for exactly that.
- A substance-only rewrite of a bloated agreed document is safe when gated
  with an explicit loss check against the prior text, with the intended
  removal categories named as out of scope for the reviewer.
- Directives that reuse an existing branch must state the local
  branch/worktree holder check and the stale-worktree remedy, not just the
  origin pin.
- For long documents, produce a rendered navigable copy before asking Dave to
  read; raw markdown in the pane fails above roughly a thousand lines.

## Candidate methodology changes

- Spec-review-cycle / review-artifact / spec-reviewer role: an explicit rule
  that findings are dispositioned in the cycle directive and review artifact,
  and that the governed document under review receives the resulting rule
  text only — no provenance tags, no changelog prose, no closed questions, no
  per-citation SHAs in the body. Phrased as input to a full review cycle.
- Directive-authoring skill (or the bin/directive TRD): add the working-tree
  precondition pair for reused branches — verify the local branch's holder,
  remove a stale worktree without --force with stop-on-failure, check out the
  existing branch without -b.
- Decision-layer or a skill: a stated form for presenting long documents for
  Dave's reading (rendered, navigable), distinct from the artifact-pane
  review of short documents.
