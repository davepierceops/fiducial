# Retro — illuminait — 2026-08-24

## Context

A discovery engagement, not a build: read another company's architecture corpus (a Quartz-published Obsidian/Karpathy-style wiki, 158 pages) and produce a customer-facing statement of what its owner can truthfully claim about air-gapped deployment. No spec existed, none was wanted. The session ran as bounded research spikes over the corpus, then one synthesized brief with Core's evidence labelling, then this retro. Kickoff explicitly framed it as the first deliberate instance of the brownfield/SRE mode fiducial does not yet have.

## Evidence

1. The kickoff block said "ask Dave for the docs before asserting anything" and "ask how, one question." Getting the docs took five turns and four mechanisms: a PDF one-pager (marketing), a screenshot of the wiki, a failed direct fetch (not indexed), a failed Chrome-extension read (not connected), then an RSS file (titles only), then the wiki's search index JSON — which contained the full text of every page. Dave did not know the last one existed; the CoS did, from knowing how Quartz sites are built.
2. Dave's stated constraint on acquisition: "I can ask Matt for a ZIP if I really need to, but I feel like he's gonna believe that he already gave me access. I'd rather just figure out how to get it ourselves." The relationship shaped the acquisition method.
3. The first document that arrived (the one-pager) was, in Dave's words, "the least useful document" and "the easiest to find." It was still used: as the baseline of what the owner already says publicly, against which the brief had to be consistent.
4. First spike was a corpus-wide grep for the topic terms (`air.?gap|sovereign|on-prem|offline|self-host|egress|disconnected`). It returned 60 of 158 pages by filename and, on the narrower `air.?gap|sovereign` grep with line context, surfaced the central finding in one screen: two pages dated 2026-08-17 call air-gapped/sovereign "production-built today, not roadmap" while the engineering status page, the product page, the competitor table, and a June call note all say "Planned P1 Wave 1" / "on roadmap."
5. The corpus had already done a large part of the work. The engineering status page carries a dated "Ground-Truth Correction (2026-07-28)" section that explicitly greps its own doc set ("confirmed by grep -rn -i 'ebpf' across all 15 pages, zero hits") and flags GTM claims that outrun engineering. The Arkansas fit analysis (2026-08-21) has a "Where Caution Is Required" section. The retro author's job was mostly to *find* and *join* these, not to originate them.
6. The corpus also contained internal IP addresses, VPN peer assignments, and named contractor access details in an operations page. The brief cites that page for one fact (eBPF is in development, not shipped) and reproduces none of the infrastructure detail. No rule in the loaded methodology governs this; it was a judgment call made in passing.
7. The distinguishing move in the brief was noticing that one word ("air-gapped") was carrying two incompatible meanings across the corpus — self-hosted-in-customer-network vs. disconnected-enclave — and that the documented design for the second (store-and-forward into *Illuminait's* SaaS) inverts what the buyer means. No single page states this; it appears only when the engineering §2 constraint, the product deployment table, and the Investment Thesis wedge are read together.
8. Evidence labelling (observed / inferred / told / unknown) was applied per claim. The "unknown" class did the most work: seven of the brief's items are questions for engineering, each stating what would change if answered. The "told" class needed a sub-note ("a partner's assessment relayed in call notes, not an engineering statement") because the source's own provenance was second-hand.
9. Spikes were bounded by grep result sets and a small number of full-page reads (Platform Implementation Status, Technical Architecture index, targeted sections of five AISPM pages, Investment Thesis §, Arkansas §, Viatel call notes). Roughly 15 of 158 pages were read in full or in large part. The rest were touched only by grep. No sweep was performed; the brief says so in its "does NOT establish" section.
10. Total elapsed from first doc to both artifacts: one session, roughly 12 tool calls of extraction/grep plus two file writes. No directive, no execution session, no execution block, no committed artifact.
11. The loaded methodology bundle (Core, Decision Layer, Review Rubric, Spec and Change Discipline, Operating Model, CoS role, Commit policy, four skills) was consulted for evidence rules and register only. Nothing in it addressed: how to acquire a corpus you don't control, how to bound a spike, when a spike is done, how to record which pages were read vs. grepped, or what the deliverable of a discovery engagement is.
12. The CoS role file's read-sequence (OPEN-ITEMS, git log, pending gates) and its decomposition procedure (PRD/TRD → tranches → packages) did not apply. There was no repo, no default branch, no spec, no tracker. The role's "assess state, render it, propose next steps" activation behaviour was the only part that transferred, and it transferred to a system the CoS had no read access to until turn six.
13. The Standard Response Shape (Role / Intent / Evidence / Boundary / Gaps / Recommendation / Dave decision points) from the Operating Model maps almost exactly onto the brief's sections (bottom line / can say / must not say / need to verify / does not establish / suggested language) without having been consulted while writing them.

## Interpretation

- (1, 2) **Acquisition is a phase of brownfield work, and it is not trivial.** It consumed a third of the session's turns, required environment-specific knowledge (how Quartz publishes), and was constrained by a relationship rather than a technical limit. Greenfield fiducial assumes the artifacts are in the repo; brownfield starts before that assumption holds.
- (3) **Low-value artifacts still set the consistency baseline.** The marketing one-pager was the only document the customer will actually see, so the brief had to not contradict it or explain where it does.
- (4, 7) **The productive unit was "one grep, one screen, one finding," not "read the docs."** Bounded spikes worked because they were term-driven and the corpus was text. A corpus that is code, or logs, or a running system, would need a different spike primitive.
- (5) **The target system had its own evidence discipline.** The corpus already contained dated corrections, self-greps, and flagged overclaims. Brownfield discovery on a well-kept corpus is largely *joining* — finding the pages that disagree and stating the disagreement. Core rule 9 ("two sources disagree → surface it") was the single most-used rule, and the corpus's own dated-correction convention made it possible to say *which* statement is newer without treating newer as truer.
- (6) **There is no rule about not carrying the target's secrets back out.** Core rule 1 covers secret *values*; it doesn't cover infrastructure detail in a customer's corpus that the discovery artifact should not reproduce. It happened to be handled; it was not governed.
- (8) **"Unknown" is the load-bearing class in discovery.** In greenfield, unknowns are gaps in a spec to be closed before handoff. Here, the enumerated unknowns *are* a primary deliverable — the list of questions that convert "architecture claim" into "track record." The evidence rules carried over unchanged, but their emphasis inverted.
- (9, 11) **Spike boundaries and coverage were improvised.** Nothing said when a spike was done or how to record what was and wasn't read. The brief's "does NOT establish" section is the only record of coverage, and it exists because Core rule 8 forbids claiming completeness without a sweep — a rule that was honoured by disclaiming rather than by sweeping.
- (10, 12) **The decision/execution split did not fire.** All work happened in the decision session because there was nothing to execute against and no repo to commit to. The methodology's flow (decision → directive → execution → report → verify → merge) had no entry point. Whether that is fine for a one-evening spike or a gap that bites on a multi-week SRE engagement is not settled by one instance.
- (13) **The Operating Model's response shape already fits discovery output.** It was not used, and the brief converged on it anyway. That suggests the shape is more general than the greenfield flow around it.

## Durable insights

- A published knowledge base built with a static-site generator usually ships its full text as a search index; that single file is the whole corpus. This generalizes to acquisition on other engagements.
- Term-driven corpus greps with line context, run before any full-page read, find the contradictions faster than reading does, and the contradictions are where the findings are.
- A well-kept target corpus with dated corrections is the best brownfield evidence there is; the discovery job becomes joining pages, and "newer" must not be promoted to "truer."
- In discovery, the enumerated-unknowns list is a first-class deliverable, not a residue.
- Discovery output has a stable shape — bottom line / can say / must not say / need to verify / does not establish — and it is the Operating Model's response shape under another name.

## Candidate methodology changes

Phrased as inputs to a future review cycle, not decisions. Consistent with the kickoff instruction, no design is proposed here — only the gaps the evidence names.

- A brownfield/discovery mode needs an **acquisition phase** as a named step with its own evidence rules (what was obtained, how, from whom, what was refused), preceding any read.
- A **spike** needs a definition: its bounding term or question, its stopping condition, and a record of coverage (pages read in full / touched by grep / untouched) that the deliverable can cite instead of disclaiming.
- A rule scoped like Core 1 but for **the target's internal detail** — infrastructure addresses, access arrangements, named third parties — stating what a discovery artifact may carry out of the corpus it was derived from.
- The evidence classes should say explicitly that in discovery, **`unknown` is a deliverable** and each unknown states what would change if resolved.
- The CoS role's read-sequence and activation behaviour need a stated variant for **no-repo, no-spec engagements**, or a statement that a different role applies.
- Whether discovery work requires the **decision/execution split** at all, and at what size of engagement it starts to, is an open question this session cannot answer; a second instance should be run with that question live.
