# Retro — fiducial — 2026-08-23

## Context
One decision session ran Pass 1 from cycle 16 through 24: eight policies, eleven roles, nine skills, two spec templates, two vendor files, four engagement files. Nine review directives, five revision directives, four follow-ons; 27 commits on main; seven files retired, two created. Every directive and artifact is on main.

## Evidence
1. Parallel review sessions were run in one clone four times. Cycles 16 and 19 collided (one commit landed on the other session's branch; both recovered by rebuilding). Cycle 20's session stopped at the first foreign file per a working-tree rule written into its directive; the 21b second run found its own prior run and refused to clobber it.
2. The assistant twice gave the separate-clone instruction in chat rather than in the directive; Dave pointed out chat instructions do not reach the executor.
3. The assistant told an executor to revert a file it had not authored (.claude/settings.json). The file was Dave's intentional change from another session. Recovered from the commit that swept it in.
4. The assistant handed Dave a block to assemble from parts; Dave required a complete paste block.
5. Dave ran a directive twice (21b); the second session diffed against the first and stopped.
6. Dave lost the open question across a double report; restating it took two sentences.
7. Executors varied from finding Fixes 31 times across the five revisions; every variance reported with a reason; all accepted on inspection. Two were the executor being more right than the finding (D6 conditions; ER-1 three-way recommendation split).
8. The Context Quality Reviewer role was nearly merged into the rubric; Dave's objection — instruction-file review is a distinct discipline from spec review — reversed it.
9. gh failed authentication in every execution session; curl against the REST API and the browser compare URL both worked every time.
10. The Release Manager's "needs Dave decision" survived as ship-call vocabulary while the same words were banned as a gap label; two executors independently drew the line correctly.

## Interpretation
- (1, 2) The directive is the only channel to an executor. A rule that matters to execution goes in the directive file or does not exist.
- (3) "Report, don't recover" is the executor's rule; the assistant applied its own judgment to a file outside the cycle's scope and was wrong. Foreign changes are Dave's to disposition.
- (4) Assembly is the assistant's job. A block with a blank is not a block.
- (5, 7) Executors are reliable on provenance and on Fix-vs-rule conflicts; directives can state rules and precedence and let the executor resolve.
- (6) After two reports in one turn, restate the open question before anything else.
- (8) Criterion-10 merges need a second question: is the merge target the same kind of artifact? A role and a rubric are not.

## Durable insights
- Directives carry every rule the executor needs, including environment facts (branch from SHA, REST not gh, scratchpad clone).
- Never disposition a foreign change in an executor's tree; surface it to Dave.
- Restate the open question after every multi-report turn.
- A session that has absorbed ten directives and a dozen artifacts should hand off before the next directory, not after.

## Candidate methodology changes
- chief-of-staff.md: "A change in an executor's tree that the session did not make is reported to Dave; the Chief of Staff does not direct its reversal."
- directive-authoring.md: environment facts the executor needs are stated in the directive.
- Core rule 14 candidate clarification: retro and baton filenames share one timestamp when committed together.
