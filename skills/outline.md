# Skill: Outline

Run this in a Writer session when the author asks for an outline. Read the
Public Prose Criteria and the Voice document in this bundle first.

## What an outline is

An outline is the piece's contract with itself: what it claims, how firmly,
and where. Produce it as one document in the document pane, in this order.
The first part is worked in chat with the author before the document exists.
Parts 1 through 3 are the outline; produce part 4 only when the author asks
for it.

## 1. Topics

Inventory every topic or area of discussion in the source material — the
notes, the braindump, the conversation — as a flat numbered list, one line
each, in the order they appeared. Leave nothing out and rank nothing.

Present the list and ask the author which few are the piece's top-level
topics. Importance is the author's call; the inventory is yours. With the
top-level topics named, propose where each remaining topic sits beneath
them, or that it drops, and present that grouping for correction. The
approved grouping is the skeleton the section plan fills in: top-level topics
become sections; subordinate topics become what a section argues.

## 2. Thesis

One sentence. What the piece asserts, at the tier the piece will earn for it.
If the author has not stated the thesis, propose one from the conversation
and ask; do not proceed to the claims list on a thesis the author has not
accepted.

## 3. Claims list

Every claim the piece will make, numbered, one line each, with its tier from
the Criteria's claims taxonomy — relayed, demonstrated, grounded, opinion —
stated on the line. Assign the tier the author's evidence earns, not the tier
the argument wants. Where you cannot tell what the evidence is, mark the tier
*unknown* and ask.

The claims list is the reference the Copy Editor's claims-tier audit and the
Critic's Skeptic pass compare the finished prose against. A claim the prose
makes that is absent here is a finding; a claim here the prose drops is a
finding.

## 4. Section plan — on request only

Produce this part only when the author asks for a section plan. Without one,
the Writer drafts against the approved topic grouping, the thesis, and the
claims list. When asked, one entry per section, in reading order, sections
following the approved topic grouping:

- title
- what the section argues, in one or two sentences
- which claims, by number, it carries
- a word budget

Take the total word target from the author. Sum the section budgets to the
target, and state the sum. Leave no claim on the list uncarried by a section
and give no section a claim the list does not hold.

## Approval

Present the outline and stop. The author approves, reorders, merges, splits,
or drops; the approved outline is the one the Writer drafts against. Record
any tier the author changes as the author's assignment.

## Constraints

- Propose; the author decides thesis, tiers, order, and budgets.
- Where the Voice document sets a structural convention, follow it; the
  section plan is otherwise the piece's own.
